# ☕ Java Relational Operators:

## 1. What are Relational Operators?

In simple English, **relational operators** are used to **compare two values**. 

Think about real life: 
* Is my age **greater than** your age? 
* Is the price of this laptop **equal to** the price of that phone?

Whenever you compare two things, the answer is always **Yes** or **No**. 
In Java, "Yes" is called `true`, and "No" is called `false`. This type of Yes/No data is called a **boolean**.

---

## 2. The Six Relational Operators in Java

Here is a visual map of the operators you will use:

```text
    [ Value 1 ]  ====( compares to )====>  [ Value 2 ]
                       |
                       +--->  ==  (Is equal to?)
                       |
                       +--->  !=  (Is NOT equal to?)
                       |
                       +--->  >   (Is greater than?)
                       |
                       +--->  <   (Is less than?)
                       |
                       +--->  >=  (Is greater than OR equal to?)
                       |
                       +--->  <=  (Is less than OR equal to?)
```

### 📊 Summary Table
| Operator | Name | Example (if A = 10, B = 20) | Result |
| :---: | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `==` | Equal to | `A == B` (Is 10 equal to 20?) | `false` |
| `!=` | Not equal to | `A != B` (Is 10 NOT equal to 20?)| `true` |
| `>` | Greater than | `A > B` (Is 10 greater than 20?) | `false` |
| `<` | Less than | `A < B` (Is 10 less than 20?) | `true` |
| `>=` | Greater than or equal to | `A >= B` (Is 10 greater/equal to 20?) | `false` |
| `<=` | Less than or equal to | `A <= B` (Is 10 less/equal to 20?) | `true` |

> **⚠️ Big Developer Warning!**
> Do not confuse `=` with `==`. 
> * A single `=` is used to **give** a value (e.g., `int age = 18;`).
> * A double `==` is used to **compare** values (e.g., `age == 18`).
